## 2. What the exam rewards (read from the 2024–25 markschemes in the folder)

Current syllabus (first exams 2024): **P1** pre-released case, 30 marks, one 10-marker from a
choice of two. **P2** HL quantitative, 50 marks, with a 10-marker at the end of the chosen Section B
question. **P3** HL social enterprise, 25 marks, ending in a 17-mark plan of action.

The 10-mark markband (identical in every 2024–25 P1/P2 markscheme) has four strands: **demands of
the question**, **BM tools/theories**, **use of the stimulus**, and **substantiated, balanced
arguments**. The per-question notes add rules that aren't visible from the band alone:

- **Top band [9–10] needs limitations.** You need "reference to the limitations of the case study":
  missing information, unreliable data, short time frame. Most students never write this.
- **One-sided caps at [4].** (2024 May P2, Avuela.)
- **No judgement caps at [7–8]** on "recommend / should they" questions. Balance without a decision
  only partly answers the question.
- **Not using the named table/figure caps at [6].** (2024 Nov P2, Chaltex.)
- **SWOT/STEEPLE.** The P3 markscheme (2025 Nov) says a "descriptive plan of action based around a
  SWOT or STEEPLE which merely copies or re-purposes the stimulus" is **inappropriate**: criteria A
  and D are cut, and a SWOT that only lifts the stimulus gets 1 mark for resource use. Frameworks
  earn marks only when they do analytical work. Use Ansoff when the question names it, force-field
  when it's asked for, a calculated ratio when there's a table. **This answers her question directly.**
- **Short answers need application.** In a 4-mark "explain one advantage and one disadvantage",
  each half is capped at [1] if it doesn't refer to the case. A 2-mark "state" needs no explanation at all, so writing one wastes time.

### The skeleton to teach her (built from those rules, not from a generic essay template)

For "Discuss / Recommend / Evaluate whether X should do Y" (about 25 min in P1, about 20 in P2):

1. **Frame (2 lines).** Define the key term and restate the decision in the case's own words.
2. **Two arguments for, then two against.** Each one runs: point → BM theory/tool → **case
   evidence (numbers, line refs)** → consequence for an objective (profit, reputation, staff).
   That "because → so → which means" chain is the analysis.
3. **One number worked out from the data** (margin, % change, payback, gearing) whenever there's a
   table.
4. **Limitations.** What the case doesn't tell us and why that matters.
5. **Judgement.** Answer the question. Say which argument outweighs the others and why, and what the
   decision depends on.

Sentence labels for feedback (this tackles "what counts as analysis"):
**Knowledge** (defines/states) · **Application** (uses case facts) · **Analysis** (cause → effect on
the business) · **Evaluation** (weighs, judges, notes limits).

## 3. Studying for the mock (assuming Tuesday 13 Oct, so 3 days)

Research consensus (Dunlosky et al. 2013 and later work): **practice testing and spaced practice**
are the high-utility techniques. Re-reading, highlighting and summarising are low utility. For
business that means **writing timed answers to real prompts and getting marked feedback**, not
making notes. Keep sessions short, with one task each.

| Day | Session (25–40 min each) |
|---|---|
| **Sat/Sun** | (1) Learn the skeleton and the cap rules from **one annotated example**: the examiner's "possible answer" in the 2024 May P2 Avuela markscheme, labelled sentence by sentence. (2) Timed 10-marker: a Myt 2025 or WW 2025 growth decision → feedback → **rewrite the weakest paragraph** (the rewrite is where the learning happens) |
| **Sun/Mon** | (3) Timed 10-marker on HR (motivation or industrial relations, P2 style with a table). (4) 20-min drill: 2-mark definitions + P2 calculations (ratios, depreciation, decision trees, investment appraisal). These are the cheap marks: most of P1/P2 is short-answer |
| **Mon** | (5) **P3: plan only** (15-min outline against criteria A–D, not a full write-up) → feedback. (6) Re-read her own "mistakes I keep making" list, built from the feedback. Then stop and sleep |

Pick prompts from the **current-syllabus table** in essay-prompts.md. Start with growth/strategy
decisions, which are about 9 of the 14 recent P1 ten-markers. **If her school's mock P1 uses a
known pre-released statement, that changes everything:** she should practise only on that company,
learn its terms and numbers, and we'd write prompts that anticipate it. The folder has the
pre-releases for Nov 2024 (OWL), May 2025 (Myt) and Nov 2025 (WW). Myt alone has 6 real
ten-markers across 3 time zones.

## 4. The essay feedback loop (your idea), and the catch about sample essays

**Flow:** pick a topic → pick time → she gets the case (whole P1 case, or the one P2 stimulus) plus
the prompt → writes it by hand on iPad (the real exam is handwritten) or types it → sends it →
gets feedback → rewrites one paragraph → feedback is logged.

**Feedback she gets, in this order:**
1. Estimated band per strand, and **which cap rules she hit** ("no limitations → can't reach 9–10").
2. Markscheme indicative points she used and missed.
3. Her essay with **every sentence labelled** K / App / An / Ev, so missing analysis is visible.
4. Her weakest paragraph rewritten one level up, next to her version.
5. One specific thing to do next time. Recurring issues across essays become her personal checklist.

**About "actual IB essays":** I looked, and real IB-scored candidate scripts aren't public. IB's
subject reports and annotated scripts sit behind My IB / the Programme Resource Centre, which only
schools can access. Web "model answers" (Save My Exams, RevisionDojo, etc.) aren't IB-scored, and
some give advice that conflicts with the markschemes (e.g. "cover 4–5 points" when the bands reward
depth and judgement). What's authentic and already on disk:
- markbands plus question-specific indicative content and cap rules for every prompt;
- **examiner-written "possible answers"** in some markschemes: 2024 May P2 (Avuela), and full sample
  plans in the 2024 May, 2025 May and 2025 Nov P3 markschemes;
- **ask her teacher** for the May/Nov 2025 subject reports and any annotated exemplars. Teachers
  have access, and this is the one thing worth her asking for.

Model answers we generate get labelled as generated. To keep the AI marker honest, calibrate it first:
mark the examiner "possible answers" (should land 9–10) and a deliberately one-sided version (should
cap at 4) before trusting it on her work. Her teacher-marked mock is the real check afterwards.
